# Sampling, Bias, and Uncertainty

**Task ID:** `da1t2-003`  
**Estimated effort:** 10 hours  
**Module:** inference

## Why this task exists

A sample is not the population. Practice saying what you do not know, and put an interval or a resampling argument on a simple estimate.

This is an apprenticeship task, not a content-consumption checkbox. Reading or watching material is only preparation.
Completion requires evidence that you can apply and explain the ideas.

## Authoritative resources

- **OpenIntro Statistics**: https://www.openintro.org/book/os/
- **Introduction to Statistical Learning (Python)**: https://www.statlearning.com/

Use the linked course/documentation as the primary source. You may use additional sources, but record them in your
learning notes and prefer primary documentation over tutorial aggregation sites.

## Work to complete

1. State a target population and how the dataset is actually sampled.
2. Bootstrap or otherwise resample one mean or proportion and report an interval.
3. Name one selection bias and one measurement bias that could still remain.
4. Write a claim with a hedge that matches the interval — and a claim that would overreach.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Population versus sample is explicit.
- [ ] The interval method is named and implemented in code.
- [ ] No causal language unless the design supports it (it almost certainly does not).
- [ ] Bias discussion is about this dataset, not a generic list.

The mentor may request a live explanation, a change to the analysis, or a failure demonstration before approval.
A pretty notebook alone is not proof of understanding.

## Reflection

Answer these in your own words after doing the work:

1. What would a larger n not fix?
2. When is a confidence interval the wrong comfort?

Also record:

- What took longer than expected?
- What would you practice again?
- What remains unclear?
- Which artifact best proves that you learned the objective?
