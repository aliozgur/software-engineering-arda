# Descriptive Statistics You Can Defend

**Task ID:** `da1t1-004`  
**Estimated effort:** 8 hours  
**Module:** statistics

## Why this task exists

Mean, median, and a chart are not a finding. Choose summaries that match the distribution and say what they hide.

This is an apprenticeship task, not a content-consumption checkbox. Reading or watching material is only preparation.
Completion requires evidence that you can apply and explain the ideas.

## Authoritative resources

- **OpenIntro Statistics**: https://www.openintro.org/book/os/
- **Introduction to Statistical Learning (Python)**: https://www.statlearning.com/

Use the linked course/documentation as the primary source. You may use additional sources, but record them in your
learning notes and prefer primary documentation over tutorial aggregation sites.

## Work to complete

1. For one numeric variable, report mean, median, IQR, and a reason you would or would not use the mean.
2. Show a histogram or ECDF and name the shape (skew, multimodality, outliers).
3. Compare two groups without claiming significance yet.
4. Write a one-paragraph claim that is licensed by the summaries — and a sentence that would be overclaiming.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The write-up names the unit and the population the table actually covers.
- [ ] Outliers are discussed, not deleted by default.
- [ ] No p-values or 'significant' language in this task.
- [ ] A chart has a title that is a sentence, not a variable name.

The mentor may request a live explanation, a change to the analysis, or a failure demonstration before approval.
A pretty notebook alone is not proof of understanding.

## Reflection

Answer these in your own words after doing the work:

1. When would the median be the honest headline number?
2. What would make this comparison unfair?

Also record:

- What took longer than expected?
- What would you practice again?
- What remains unclear?
- Which artifact best proves that you learned the objective?

## Mentor review guide

- Ask what population the sample is not.
- Reject 'the average is X' with no distribution context.

Suggested review outcome: **Approve**, **Request revision**, or **Create follow-up challenge**. Prefer questions that
force reasoning over requests for cosmetic polish.
