# Run a 1:1 That Leaves an Agenda and a Follow-up

**Task ID:** `el1t1-002`
**Estimated effort:** 6 hours
**Module:** Mentoring

## Why this task exists

"I mentored Sam" is a claim. An agenda, notes that separate what was observed from what you inferred, and a follow-up with owners and dates are evidence. This task is the first inspectable mentoring cycle in the path: prepare, conduct (or role-play), write down what happened, send the follow-up.

This is an apprenticeship task, not a content-consumption checkbox. Reading about staff-plus mentoring is preparation. Completion requires the three artifacts.

## Authoritative resources

- **Staff Engineer (Will Larson)** (primary): https://staffeng.com/ — read the material on teaching, sponsorship, and how staff-plus engineers grow other engineers. Record any extra sources in your notes.

## Scenario

Continue as Harborline Checkout tech lead. You have a 1:1 with **Sam** (junior, 8 months). You do not need a real mentee. Role-play both sides in writing, run it with a peer, or — if you have a real report and they consent to a redacted artifact — use the real meeting and strip names/company identifiers.

**Facts you already have (treat as observed, not rumors)**

- Sam's refund-retry fix was due Friday. It is now Wednesday of the following week. No PR exists.
- Sam booked three pairing sessions with you last week and cancelled two.
- In standup Sam said they want to "learn observability" and asked to own a dashboard.
- Sam's last merged PR (invoice PDF footer) needed five review rounds, mostly missed edge cases you had named in the first review.
- Sam's written updates in the team doc are one line: "still working on retries."

**The hard topic you must not skip:** the missed deadline and the missing PR.
**The growth topic you must not skip:** Sam's interest in observability, and whether a dashboard is the right next stretch.

Do not turn the meeting into a status dump you could have read in the tracker.

## Work to complete

1. Write the agenda **before** the meeting. Choose 30 or 45 minutes. Time every topic. Minutes must sum to that length. Include:
   - one hard topic (the missed deadline / missing PR)
   - one growth topic (observability / next stretch)
   - at least one topic that is Sam's (a question you will actually ask, not a lecture slot)
2. Conduct the 1:1 as a written role-play (both voices), a live role-play with a peer, or a real consented meeting. If you role-play in writing, Sam must say at least one thing you did not script in the agenda — a surprise (for example, they were blocked on staging credentials for four days and did not escalate).
3. Write notes afterward. Mark at least one sentence `FACT:` and at least one `INTERPRETATION:`. Do not let interpretations sit unmarked ("Sam doesn't care about quality").
4. Send (or draft as if sent) a follow-up the same day. At least two actions, each with an owner and a calendar date. At least one action must be **yours** (unblock, review by a date, introduce Sam to the observability channel, cut scope — something you owe).
5. Commit agenda, notes, and follow-up as separate files. If you used a real meeting, redaction is part of the work: no personal health details, no performance-rating language you would not put in a company system.

## Required evidence

- A timed agenda for a 30- or 45-minute 1:1 whose topic times sum to the stated length, including one growth topic and one hard topic
- Meeting notes that mark at least one sentence as an observed fact and at least one sentence as an interpretation
- A follow-up note listing at least two actions, each with an owner and a date, of which at least one action is the lead's

Submit a repository URL plus a commit reference.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The agenda lists timed topics whose minutes sum to the stated 30- or 45-minute length and includes both a growth topic and a hard topic.
- [ ] The notes explicitly label at least one sentence as fact and at least one sentence as interpretation.
- [ ] The follow-up lists at least two actions with owner and date, and at least one action is assigned to the lead.

## Reflection

Answer in your own words after doing the work:

1. Which sentence in your notes was most at risk of being an unmarked interpretation, and how did you check it?
2. If Sam only completed *their* actions and you completed none of yours, what would fail next week?

Also record: what took longer than expected, what you would practice again, what remains unclear, and which artifact best proves the 1:1 happened as designed.

## Mentor review guide

- Ask the apprentice to defend the time split. If the hard topic has three minutes and "how are you" has twenty, request revision.
- Do not approve a follow-up where every action is Sam's. Mentoring that only assigns work to the junior is task-dumping.

Suggested review outcome: **Approve**, **Request revision**, or **Create follow-up challenge**.

## AI use policy

Mode: **guided**. AI may explain what belongs in a 1:1 versus a status meeting, offer hints if the hard topic is hard to phrase, and quiz you on fact-versus-interpretation. AI must not write the agenda, the Sam-side dialogue, the notes, or the follow-up for you. Disclose any material AI use with provider/model (if known), purpose, and verification.

## Completion gate

This task is not complete when you "had a good conversation." It is complete when the three artifacts exist, the surprise (if role-played) is visible in the notes, and a mentor — or your own second pass after a day — can check the criteria without asking you what was said.
